3:35 pm

Borough Market, the crawl

Closes 5:00 pm

London’s oldest food market sits under Victorian iron and the railway arches. Saturday is the liveliest day. Keep it to tasters and something sweet, because dinner is back here this evening. Split up, grab one small thing each, and swap halfway.

The crawl
  • Raclette at Kappacasein, melted cheese scraped onto potatoes. Share one portion.
  • Chorizo roll at Brindisa on the Stoney Street corner. It is a cult favourite.
  • Bread Ahead doughnut, custard or salted caramel, cut in half.
  • Monmouth Coffee on Stoney Street for a flat white to carry onwards.
  • Some hot-food stalls start packing up from about 4:30 pm, so do the hot things first. Peek up at the glass Floral Hall on Stoney Street, where dinner is.

4:45 pm · Across London Bridge

Walk north over London Bridge as the light goes gold. Look right for Tower Bridge and HMS Belfast, then up Gracechurch Street. It takes about 15 minutes.

5:05 pm

Leadenhall Market

Free

A Victorian market arcade painted maroon, cream and gold under a glass roof. It played Diagon Alley in the first Harry Potter film. On a Saturday the City’s office workers are gone, so you get it nearly to yourselves for photos.

Good to know
  • The blue door at 42 Bull’s Head Passage stood in for the Leaky Cauldron entrance.
  • The Sky Garden is a five-minute walk south down Lime Street.

II
Chapter two · 5:30 pm – 6:35 pm

Sunset at Sky Garden

A jungle under glass at the top of the Walkie-Talkie, timed so the sun sets halfway through.

5:30 pm

Sky Garden

Free · book 5:30 pm

The top three floors of 20 Fenchurch Street are a huge glass dome full of terraced gardens, with London all round you. Sunset is at 6:03 pm, so a 5:30 pm slot shows the city in daylight, then gold, then lights coming on across the river and up the Shard.

Good to know
  • Entry is free, but you need a timed ticket from skygarden.london. Weekend hours are 11:00 am to 9:00 pm, and a ticket gives you one hour inside.
  • If no tickets show online, walk-ins are let in when there is space. Weekends are busy, so book first.
  • Expect airport-style security and an ID check, so bring photo ID.
  • The site says one of the visitor lifts is out of service, so arrive a few minutes early.

6:35 pm · Back over London Bridge

Walk down Gracechurch Street and over London Bridge, about 15 minutes. It’s dark by now, so Tower Bridge is lit up on your left and the Shard glows straight ahead.

III
Chapter three · 6:50 pm – 8:15 pm

Dinner over the market

A proper sit-down meal in a glass hall above Borough Market, five minutes from the train home.

6:50 pm

Dinner at Roast

Book 6:45 pm

Roast sits up in the Floral Hall, a Victorian glass and iron portico that was moved here from Covent Garden. Its tall windows look down over the market below. It cooks British food built around the market’s own produce and, as the name says, it is known for its roasts, with seasonal starters and a good pudding. It’s smart casual, calm, and the nicest room by the station.

Good to know
  • Book on OpenTable for 6:45 pm. It has around 11,800 reviews there, and diners keep mentioning the setting and the market views.
  • Expect about £40 or more each. A 2 or 3 course set menu (£35 or £39) runs from September to November, but it may be lunch only, so ask.
  • Address: The Floral Hall, Stoney Street, SE1 1TL, entered from the market side.
  • Back-up: Arabica, a Levantine meze place under the arches in the same market (020 3011 5151).
